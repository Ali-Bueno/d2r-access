# Diablo II Resurrected Access

## Mod overview and control summary

### Mod by Alisson, documentation written by BFBeast666

### Conventions

Every table gives the button on a standard Xbox One/Series pad, the button on a PlayStation 4/5 pad,
and the key on the keyboard.

| Xbox | PlayStation | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Directional pad | Directional buttons | the cross-shaped pad under the left thumb |
| Left stick/Right stick | Left stick/Right stick | The joysticks, abbreviated LS/RS |
| A, B, X, Y | Cross, Circle, Square, Triangle | the so-called "face buttons" on the right side of the controller |
| RB/LB | R1/L1 | the upper two shoulder buttons, used to tab through various menus |
| RT/LT | R2/L2 | the lower two, curved shoulder buttons, also used to tab through various menus |
| L3 | L3 | pressing in the left joystick |
| R3 | R3 | pressing in the right joystick |
| Menu | Options | The small button in the right half of the controller's center |
| View | Create (PS5), Share (PS4) | The small button in the left half of the controller's center |

"Press" means a normal, quick tap of the button.

"Long-press" means "hold until the game does something". Long-press actions are usually confirmed
with some audio feedback, like the clink of coins when selling an item for example.

The keyboard has no Mod key and no long presses: each mod command has a key of its own, mostly with
Ctrl or Shift, and in menus the A key is the mouse's left click and D its right click, with Shift,
Ctrl or Alt where the game asks for a modified click. Every key is listed in the Keyboard section
of the package's `README.html`.

### The audio loot filter:

By default, all items dropped by enemies or from chests loop their drop sound. Since loot is plentiful
in Diablo II, this can get very unwieldy very quickly. In the mod settings/Loot Filter options, you can
specify which kind of sounds are NOT looped when the filter is engaged. You can filter in broad
categories (potions, gems/runes and equipment) and further fine-tune dropped equipment loop sound
behavior based on the item's rarity. All filters are off by default and need to be manually
configured. A sensible configuration for people halfway through Act I would be "Potions: off,
equipment — magic". All rarity filters are "current rarity and everything above". Silver
(socketed/ethereal) items are never filtered. And to repeat — the "filtered" items are not hidden or
removed, you can still pick them up when walking over them. This is very useful knowledge when you're
running out of potions and can replenish your stock from the world.

### Potions and the belt

The belt initially holds 4 potions of any kind and items for the "Belt" equipment slot bought from
vendors can add more rows. That means you still only have four belt slots, but each slot can have up
to four total uses. The game groups similar potions together per slot. So if you have a health potion
in slot 1 and room for more, the game will put the next healing potion you decide to pick up into slot
one as well. The game fills empty belt slots with the next potion you pick up, no matter if it's an
antidote, a stamina potion or a precious Full Rejuvenation potion.

### Player menu/Skill trees:

| Action | Xbox | PlayStation | Keyboard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Move through skill nodes | Directional pad | Directional buttons | Arrows |
| Select a skill to learn | Long-press the A button | Long-press the Cross button | A |
| Assign to skill bar | Long-press the X button | Long-press the Square button | S or Shift+S opens the right or left skill list, then F1 to F8 on the skill |

Diablo II Resurrected has a very flexible controller layout — you can freely decide which of your
buttons fire off which skills. After learning a new active skill, you NEED to assign it to one of the
available skill bar buttons. Long-pressing X (Square) on an active skill opens a new window and by
pressing any of the A, B, X, Y, RB or RT buttons (Cross, Circle, Square, Triangle, R1 or R2) you will
assign the skill to that button which represent your first skill bar. Some builds, especially those
using summons, often need more buttons. D2R has a second skill bar, accessible when holding LT (L2).
Skills on Bar 2 are used by holding LT and pressing the A, B, X, Y, RB and RT buttons.

On the keyboard there are no skill bars: A on a skill in the tree learns it, and in a skill list
(S for the right hand, Shift+S for the left) F1 to F8 bind the skill under the cursor to that key.
In play those keys choose the skill, and A (left) or W (right) uses it.

Besides each character's three skill trees, D2R also has a fourth page where you can find so-called
"general" skills like Basic Attack and Open Town Portal. These general skills can be rebound at any
time. Some only work with particular pieces of equipment — setting "Throw" to a button and not having
a throwable weapon equipped does nothing, also "Loot to Cube" only works when you have a Horadric
Cube in your inventory.

### Player menu/inventory, shop and stash management

The player inventory contains two sections — the equipment portion which holds equipment slots
(weapons, armor, head gear, belt, boots, two rings and one amulet) and a 4 rows by 10 columns sized
inventory which holds unequipped items, charms and certain consumables.

Shopkeepers and the stash have several pages of windows, use RT and LT (R2 and L2; Page Up and Page
Down on the keyboard) when paging through their stock or the different stash tabs. These windows
behave exactly like the player inventory, only they are substantially larger at 10 rows by 10
columns.

#### In player inventory:

| Action | Xbox | PlayStation | Keyboard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Move focus | Directional pad | Directional buttons | Arrows |
| Pick up, put down item | Press A button on item/another item/inventory/stash/vendor cell | Press Cross button on item/another item/inventory/stash/vendor cell | A |

Putting down an item in an equipment slot equips it, removing it unequips. Putting down an item in an
occupied equipment slot or inventory slot exchanges what you are currently "holding" with the item
you're hovering over. You can manually place potions of any kind in any belt slot. Picking up an item
in your inventory and moving into a shop window and putting it down will sell it. Picking up a gem,
jewel or rune and placing it in an item with open sockets will socket it. THIS IS IRREVERSIBLE (short
of a very expensive late-game recipe which only leaves the base item, BUT NOT THE SOCKETABLES behind).

#### Stash operations:

| Action | Xbox | PlayStation | Keyboard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Page through stash tabs | RT/LT | R2/L2 | Page Down/Page Up |
| Page through shared stash sub-pages | RB/LB | R1/L1 | Alt+Page Down/Alt+Page Up (Ctrl+Shift+Page Down/Up: ten pages) |
| Move focus | Directional pad | Directional buttons | Arrows |
| Pick up, drop | Press A button | Press Cross button | A |
| Quick-move item between inventory and the currently open stash window | Long-press X button | Long-press Square button | Ctrl+A |
| Quick-sort gems, runes and materials into stash | Press Y button with any open stash window | Press Triangle button with any open stash window | — |

Quick-sorted items go into their fitting tabs (gems, runes, materials, which also includes
Rejuvenation potions).

The stash is split into five distinct tabs with unique functionality. The leftmost one is the Personal
tab. Any item and gold placed in it are accessible ONLY to the character that put them there. The
Personal tab is the only one in which you can store quest items like the parts of the Horadric Staff,
Khalim's Organs or Wirt's Leg. No other stash tab will accept these items. Also, gold placed in this
stash tab (and this stash tab ONLY) will be used when buying items from shops in town. It also will be
used when a character dies and Death claims a portion of that character's wealth.

The Shared stash tab has five pages and its own, separate gold receptacle. These five pages can be
accessed from any character on the same account and it is good practice to dedicate each page to a
particular use case (one page as an item transfer space where useful items for your other chars can
be moved to and from, one page for rings, amulets and jewels, one for set items, one for other
valuable loot and so forth). Gold placed here will not be used to pay vendors in town when player gold
runs out, but it ALSO will not be taxed by Death if a character dies.

The gem, material and rune tabs hold their respective items, sorted by quality or rune type. Be aware
that there is a cap of 99 for each stack in these tabs. So you can only stash a maximum of 99 chipped
topazes, 99 minor rejuvenation potions or 99 Ral runes. Given the way D2's economy works with runes
and gems, you will rarely hit that cap aside from Rejuvenation potions, which you will amass quite
quickly if you're frugal with them. Besides allowing for quick-sorting, these three stash tabs have
built-in Horadric Cube functionality if the player has one in their inventory.

### The Horadric Cube

During the events of Act II you will receive an item called the Horadric Cube. It is one of the most
useful things a character will get their hands on. Cain will give you a basic run-down on what you
can do with it, but the crafting rabbit hole goes much, much deeper.

In general, operating the cube works much like handling the stash or a vendor window, with some
added comfort features.

have the cube open right on their page, facilitating quick transmutation of gems, materials and
runes.
